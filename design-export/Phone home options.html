The phone's home: Today and the widgets

Three directions for the first screen of the phone app, each with a matching family of Android home-screen widgets. They're drawn on the same day (lectures, Deep Learning study, the gym, the goal of the day), on a 360, a 390 and a 430 wide phone, and the widgets sit on a real-looking home screen. Use the toggle for day and night (the widgets follow the phone's dark mode). Every direction keeps the paper goal card (smaller), the Yesterday / Tomorrow day stepper, Today's tasks apart from Also open, and the morning and evening rituals. The colours are the app's own, with the calendar's palette C for the blocks. Answer with letters, for example "phone B, widgets B". The desktop version is in Today options.html.

What Android widgets can and can't do

  • They're built from RemoteViews, so only stock views are allowed. That means Frame, Linear, Relative and Grid layouts, plus TextView, ImageView, Button, ProgressBar, Chronometer and the collection views (ListView, GridView, StackView). There are no custom views, no blur, no live animation, and no web content. The tape, the clover and the paper have to be drawable images. The tilt is dropped, so the paper card stands straight.
  • Fonts are limited. The widgets use the system serif and sans-serif-medium today, not the app's display serif or its typewriter stamp. The ✶ Goal of the day stamp would be a small picture, or letter-spaced sans text.
  • Android 12 and later round the corners themselves. The launcher clips the widget to its own radius (system_app_widget_background_radius, about 16–28 dp depending on the launcher). Our layouts should use @android:id/background and the matching inner radius, instead of the fixed 22 dp they draw now.
  • Material You colours (Android 12+), the system_accent and system_neutral palettes, could tint a widget to match the wallpaper. These designs keep the app's own paper and ink instead: an opaque card with AA text reads on any wallpaper, light or dark. Night Garden comes from values-night, so the widgets follow the phone's dark mode (they already do).
  • Updates are rationed. updatePeriodMillis can't go below 30 minutes. Ours redraw when the app pushes a new snapshot, every 30 minutes, and with one inexact alarm when something changes (a block starts or ends, midnight). A per-minute "in 20m" goes stale between updates, so a widget shows clock times ("11:00") or uses a Chronometer, which counts down by itself.
  • Checkboxes. On Android 12 and later, a real CheckBox in RemoteViews, with setOnCheckedChangeResponse, ticks at once without opening the app. Before 12 it's an image with a PendingIntent broadcast. That's how ours work today (WidgetActions TICK: drawn at once, then the app applies it the next time it runs).
  • Sizes. targetCellWidth and targetCellHeight (Android 12+), the min and max resize sizes, and size-mapped RemoteViews let one widget change its face as it's resized (small, then medium, then large). Ours pick the face from the size the launcher reports. Lists that scroll need a collection view. The current widgets draw a fixed number of rows, and so do these.
  • The widget picker shows a preview. On Android 12 and later that can be a live previewLayout instead of a picture.
