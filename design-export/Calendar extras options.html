1 · Block colours

You said: "I don't like the colours of the blocks". Each palette is drawn on the same calendar (task, event, Time slot, the goal, done, past, "Now" and "Ran over"), by day and in Night Garden, with its swatch grid. The block's look stays as built (the colour at 24% for the fill, 45% for the edge). Every text colour is worked out so it passes AA (4.5:1) on every fill it can sit on: the plain grid, today's lavender wash, and the "Now" wash.

3 · The calendar checkbox and the repeat glyph

You find the checkbox thin and square. Three thicker, rounder ones, open and ticked, on 15-minute, 30-minute and one-hour blocks at 100%, 125% (the app's default) and 150%. The check still sits on the title's first line (the dashed line in the 150% hour block marks that line's middle). In the app the box only shows when the pointer is over the block, or once it's ticked; that stays.

The repeat glyph

A repeating task's box becomes two arrows chasing each other (Akiflow's idea). Ticking it completes this occurrence; the next one comes as the repeat says. Its name for a screen reader: "Complete this occurrence (repeats weekly)". Drawn in the kf line style (24 grid, stroke 1.6, round caps); on a block the stroke matches your checkbox pick's weight.

4 · The Ongoing / Up next card

At the bottom left of the calendar page: pinned to the foot of the "To plan" rail when it's open, floating 16px in from the grid's corner when the rail is folded, a one-line strip above the tab bar on the phone. Its rules come from the research: only things within the next hour count as "Up next"; when two run at once the one that ends soonest leads (a long block can't hide a prayer or a meeting inside it); clock times always sit under the bar, so the countdown is never the only thing it says.

5 · The Windows tray

Left-click opens a small window of ours above the K (it's already a webview, so its whole look is ours; the cost there is the signed-out bug, item 14: it must share the main window's session). Right-click opens a menu. Each scene follows the page's Day/Night switch; Windows is drawn light by day and dark at night.
