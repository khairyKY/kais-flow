# The Kai's Flow way

*First draft, 23 Oct 2026, for Kai to edit. Written for the person opening Kai's Flow for the first time. It will become the Learn lessons, the method card and the "why" lines in the app, so keep it short.*

---

Most planners teach you their buttons. This page is about the way of working that the buttons are for. It's short, and it has opinions, because a calm day mostly comes from a few small habits done every day, not from a clever system.

**In one breath:** get everything out of your head. Each morning, choose three things and give the first one a time. Work on one thing at a time. Close the day in the evening. Once a week, look over the whole garden.

## The daily loop

About ten minutes of planning in a whole day. The rest of the day is for doing.

1. **Capture everything.** *All day, a few seconds each.* The moment something crosses your mind, write it down. Tap the terra button to type, hold it to talk, or press ⌘K on a computer. Don't sort it yet: "call Omar Friday 5pm" is enough, and the date is picked out for you. A head that's holding things can't think about them.
2. **Clear the Inbox.** *Once a day, about two minutes.* Everything you didn't sort waits in the Inbox. Go through it once a day and decide each one: a task, a note, or nothing. "Nothing" is a good answer more often than you'd think. Plan my day does this for you as one of its steps.
3. **Pick a Top 3, with one goal.** *In the morning, about three minutes.* Star three things for today. The first one is the goal of the day: if only one thing gets done, it's this one. Three is plenty. Everything else is still there; it just isn't today's promise.
4. **Give it a time.** *In the morning, one minute.* A task with a time goes on the calendar, and a task with a time gets done. Give the goal your best hour, before the day fills up. For kinds of work rather than single tasks, make a Time slot, like "Study block", and drop tasks into it.
5. **One thing at a time.** *Through the day.* When it's time, open Focus, pick the task and start a round: 25 minutes on that one thing, then a short break. When the round ends, the next one is a choice, not an obligation.
6. **Close the day.** *From five in the evening, about three minutes.* Shut down: tick what's done, move what isn't to tomorrow or let it go, write one line about the day, and star tomorrow's three. Then stop. Tomorrow is already planned, so you don't have to carry it to bed.

## The weekly loop

About twenty minutes, once a week. Pick a fixed time, like Friday afternoon or Sunday evening, and put it on the calendar as a repeating block, so it happens.

**The weekly review is the sweep.** Open Review and walk through each domain, area and project:

- **What moved?** You'll see the hours by area, the focus trend and what bloomed this week.
- **What went quiet?** *Slipping* lists what you haven't touched in a while. For each one, give it a next step, put it in Someday, or let it go.
- **What's finished?** Archive it, and it's pressed into the Herbarium.
- **How did the routines go?** Look at the streaks, and keep the routines that are working.
- **What matters next week?** Choose one or two bigger things, and give them a time.

A daily plan without a weekly look turns into busywork: you do the urgent things and the important ones slip. The review is the twenty minutes that keeps the other six days honest.

## The five habits that matter most

If you keep only five things from this page, keep these. Each one has a home in the app.

| Habit | How Kai's Flow helps |
|---|---|
| **1. Write it down the moment you think of it.** | The capture button is on every page: tap to type, hold to talk. You can also use ⌘K, share from any app, the Windows tray, a home-screen widget, your capture link, or a photo of a paper list. The date, length and priority are filled in for you. |
| **2. Empty the Inbox every day.** | Each item is one key: `e` files it with the AI's suggestion, `d` dismisses it, `s` snoozes it. Plan my day has an Inbox step, so clearing it is part of the morning. |
| **3. Decide the day before it starts: three things, one goal.** | Plan my day walks you through it. The ☆ stars a task, the first one becomes the goal card, and Shut down seeds tomorrow's three the night before. |
| **4. Give important things a time.** | Drag a task onto the calendar. *Plan…* on any task finds the next free slot. Time slots hold a kind of work, and your Google calendar shows what's already taken. |
| **5. Close the day, every day.** | Shut down appears on Today from five in the evening, and an evening nudge can remind you. It takes about three minutes and ends with one line in your journal. |

## When the day goes wrong

Plans break. That's normal, and it isn't a failure.

- **A task you can't face today:** swipe it right, and it moves to tomorrow. No guilt, and Undo is right there.
- **A meeting ate your morning:** *Plan…* on the task finds it a new time.
- **You missed a few days:** don't try to catch up. Open Plan my day today, and the carry-over step asks about each late task: today, tomorrow, someday, or drop it.
- **You missed a routine:** streaks forgive one missed day a month. The vine waits for you.

## What not to do

- **Don't star everything.** A Top 3 of seven is a list, not a plan.
- **Don't plan every minute.** Leave gaps. Things always take longer, and something always comes up. Plan less than the day holds.
- **Don't organise for the sake of it.** Projects, areas, domains, labels and smart lists are there when you need them, not on day one. Start with Today and the Inbox. Add structure only when a list gets too long to read.
- **Don't keep a task alive out of guilt.** If you've moved it to tomorrow five times, it's either too big (break it into steps) or not important (let it go).
