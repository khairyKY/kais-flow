## Root cause per platform

### Android (phone)
The phone's only path is its own schedule (`notifications/plan.ts` → `android.ts` → @capacitor/local-notifications → AlarmManager). It's built from what the phone last synced, and it's re-planned only while the app runs. Three things made it miss reminders:

1. **Changes made elsewhere never reach it.** A reminder set on the PC, by Claude through MCP (the "DL: …" study tasks), or in a browser lands on the phone's schedule only the next time the phone app opens. Nothing can wake a closed Android app for a server change except FCM, and the app has no FCM.
2. **Its schedule could be wiped.** Every re-plan (each cache change, and every time the app goes to the background) cancelled *everything* pending first, then scheduled the new plan. These are separate bridge calls. If the app was swiped away or put to sleep between them, the phone was left with **nothing scheduled** until the app was opened again. Opening the app, adding a reminder and swiping it away is exactly that window.
3. **The window was 3 days.** After 3 days without opening the app, even the digest and the nudge stopped.

Also checked, and fine:
- `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` is asked once at launch and again from the test.
- `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is in the manifest (verified in the CI build log), with an inexact fallback.
- The channels are made once.
- Reboot: the plugin's `LocalNotificationRestoreReceiver` re-arms on `BOOT_COMPLETED` and skips fired one-shots.
- Snooze's `remind_again_at` (0082) is planned, keyed `id@remind_again_at`.
- `reminder_at` (tasks) and `remind_min` (events, 0077) each have their own rule, and both are planned.

The one thing code can't see is OEM battery savers. Samsung's "sleeping apps", Xiaomi Autostart and the like drop a closed app's alarms. That's now surfaced (below).

## What changed
- `notifications/android.ts` `scheduleAndroid`:
  - **Plans first, then cancels only what's no longer planned.** A same id replaces its own alarm. An interruption now leaves the old plan in place (or old plus new), never an empty one. A failed schedule cancels nothing.
  - **Capped at 400 alarms** (Android allows 500 an app); the soonest are kept.
- `tray/TrayBridge.ts`: the phone plans **7 days** ahead (was 3).

## Sounds (step 2)
**`design-export/Notification sounds options.html`** lists 28 candidates, 2–3 each, for:
- task reminder;
- event starting;
- snoozed again;
- focus round end;
- break end;
- morning digest;
- evening nudge;
- page read;
- terms changed;
- sync problem.

Each candidate has Play, a one-line description, A/B/C and "Copy my picks".

How they're made:
- `design-export/notification-sounds/make.mjs` is plain Node with no dependencies. It uses the same felt/glass voices and seeded room as `lib/sounds.ts`, all in C major pentatonic.
- Every WAV is 0.53–1.20 s, 44.1 kHz/16-bit mono, and levelled to −16 LUFS (BS.1770-4).
- True peak stays ≤ −1.1 dBTP and nothing is clipped.
- Five peak-limited ones sit at −16.8 to −17.8 LUFS. ffmpeg `ebur128` agrees within 0.5 LU.
- `node make.mjs --check` re-renders and compares byte for byte (28/28).

**Not wired in.** What each platform can do (also on the page):
- **Android:** the sound belongs to the channel, set once at creation. The build gives each picked kind a new channel with `res/raw/kf_<kind>.wav`, and deletes and replaces the old Reminders/Rituals/Focus/Quiet channels. Kai can still override any channel's sound in Android.
- **Windows:** a toast plays only `ms-winsoundevent:` sounds or packaged (Store) audio. The installer build sends the toast silent and the app plays the WAV itself (it runs whenever it toasts).
- **Web push:** the browser ignores custom sounds and uses the system sound. An open window can play the pick itself.
- **Quiet hours and Pause** silence all of them. The digest is silent today, on the Quiet channel.
